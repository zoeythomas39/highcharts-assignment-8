Female representation in government lags globally

By Zoey Thomas

Although the percent of seats held by women in government around the world has steadily increased over the last 20 years, female parlimentarians are still outnumbered by male colleagues. This trend is consistent globally, although there are some regional differences in female government representation.

Women made up the highest percent of national parliament seats in Latin America in 2023, according to data from the United Nations. They still made up less than half of representatives at 35.8%. Oceania, which includes South Pacific Ocean countries Australia and New Zealand, fared worst in 2023 at just 19.5% female parlimentarians.

Among the 10 most populated countries, Mexico was the only one to have an even ratio of men to women in government. In that country, a quota set in 2014 mandated half of all congressional candidates be women. The country is widely considered a leader in political gender equality and elected its first female president, Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo, in 2024.
